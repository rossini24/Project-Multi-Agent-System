Framing

What is an organization?

In a multi-agent system, an organization defines how autonomous agents relate to each other.

rolesauthoritycommunicationcoordinationnorms
The organizational form determines who can decide, who can request help, who can veto, and how the system responds to change.

Form 1

Centralized organization

One controller collects state, optimizes a plan, and sends instructions to agents.

  • Good: simple global optimization and conflict resolution.
  • Risk: bottleneck, single point of failure, less autonomy.

Example: a supervisor agent assigns customer-support tickets to billing, technical, legal, and escalation agents.

Form 2

Hierarchical organization

Agents are arranged in layers. Higher levels set goals; lower levels execute and report back.

  • Good: scalable delegation and clear accountability.
  • Trade-off: slower adaptation when information must climb levels.

Example: warehouse robots by zone: global scheduler → zone manager → robot navigation.

Form 3

Peer-to-peer / heterarchical organization

No permanent leader. Agents negotiate directly and coordinate through local rules.

Locality

Use neighbor information.

Resilience

Redundant paths keep the system alive.

Emergence

Global order arises locally.

Example: drones mapping a forest fire adjust sectors from nearby drones and battery status.

Form 4

Market-based organization

Agents compete or negotiate for tasks using prices, utilities, bids, or contracts.

Example: delivery robots bid for parcels based on distance, battery, load, and deadline.

Form 5

Coalition or federated organization

Separate organizations form temporary alliances to solve a shared task.

  • Good: local autonomy across departments or platforms.
  • Challenge: interoperability, trust, protocols, and conflict resolution.

Example: police, hospitals, drones, and logistics agents cooperate during a flood without merging into one central system.

Form 6

Holonic organization

A holon is simultaneously a whole and a part. Holonic MAS combine autonomy with nested coordination.

factorycellmachinetool

Example: a product holon negotiates with machine holons; each production cell also acts as a larger holon.

Comparison

Choosing an organizational form

SituationBest fitWhy
Small team, clear global objectiveCentralizedSimple control and easy debugging.
Many agents with stable responsibilitiesHierarchicalDelegation and monitoring scale naturally.
Dynamic environment, partial informationPeer-to-peerRobust local adaptation; no single failure point.
Uncertain costs or scarce resourcesMarket-basedAgents reveal preferences through bids.
Independent organizations must cooperateCoalitionShared action without loss of autonomy.
Nested physical or organizational systemsHolonicAutonomy at every level of aggregation.

Practice

Design questions

1

Where is knowledge?

Global, local, private, or distributed?

2

Who can decide?

Leader, team lead, market winner, or all peers?

3

What can fail?

Coordinator, links, incentives, or trust?

4

How does it adapt?

Replanning, negotiation, self-organization, coalition change?

Example: consider an organization for autonomous taxis during a city marathon. Which trade-offs among speed, safety, autonomy, and resilience ?

Key takeaway

Organizational structure is a coordination mechanism.

Pick the form that matches information, autonomy, uncertainty, and failure tolerance.
